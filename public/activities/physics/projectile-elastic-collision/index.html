Projectile Motion with Elastic Collision

HKAL 1987 Paper 1 — Question 5 · Interactive Teaching Page

Problem Statement

A particle is projected with a speed of 10 m s⁻¹ downwards from point P at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. The particle rebounds from the ground at point Q as shown in the figure. If the collision is perfectly elastic and the ground is smooth, what is the horizontal distance QR when it reaches the ground at R?

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning. Use the Hint buttons for extra guidance.

The particle is projected at v₀ = 10 m/s at 30° below the horizontal.

v₀ₓ = v₀ cos 30° = 10 × (√3 / 2) = 5√3 m/s ≈ 8.66 m/s

v₀ᵧ = v₀ sin 30° = 10 × (1/2) = 5 m/s (downward)

The horizontal component remains constant (no air resistance), while the vertical component accelerates downward due to gravity.

💡 Think of splitting the velocity vector into a horizontal (cos) and vertical (sin) component. The angle is measured below horizontal, so the vertical component points downward.

Use v² = u² + 2as for the vertical motion from P (height 10 m) to the ground Q.

u = 5 m/s (downward), a = g = 10 m/s², s = 10 m

v² = 5² + 2 × 10 × 10 = 25 + 200 = 225

v = 15 m/s (downward)

Time from P to Q: v = u + at → 15 = 5 + 10t → t = 1 s

💡 The particle starts 10 m above the ground with a downward velocity of 5 m/s. Gravity accelerates it further. Use the kinematic equation that relates velocity, acceleration, and displacement.

Horizontal velocity is constant (smooth ground = no friction, no air resistance):

PQ = v₀ₓ × t = 5√3 × 1 = 5√3 m ≈ 8.66 m

💡 Horizontal motion has zero acceleration, so distance = velocity × time. We already know v₀ₓ and t from the previous steps.

Perfectly elastic + smooth ground means:

  • Speed is preserved (kinetic energy conserved)
  • Vertical component reverses direction (bounces upward)
  • Horizontal component is unchanged (smooth ground → no friction)

After collision at Q:

vₓ = 5√3 m/s (unchanged, horizontal)

vᵧ = 15 m/s (upward, reversed from 15 m/s downward)

💡 "Perfectly elastic" means no kinetic energy is lost, so the speed remains 15 m/s after the bounce. Since the floor is smooth (no friction), only the vertical velocity is affected — it reverses. The horizontal component stays the same.

The particle launches upward at 15 m/s vertically and returns to ground level at R. Using s = ut + ½at² with s = 0 (back to ground):

0 = 15t − ½(10)t²

0 = 15t − 5t² = t(15 − 5t)

t = 0 (at Q) or t = 3 s (at R)

Alternatively, time to reach max height = 15/10 = 1.5 s, then falls back = 1.5 s. Total = 3 s.

💡 When the particle returns to the same level it was launched from (ground), the displacement is zero. Factor out t to find the two solutions: t = 0 (launch) and t = the answer you need.

Horizontal velocity is constant at 5√3 m/s, and the time from Q to R is 3 s:

QR = vₓ × t = 5√3 × 3 = 15√3 m

QR ≈ 25.98 m ≈ 26.0 m

Answer: C. 26.0 m

💡 √3 ≈ 1.732, so 15 × 1.732 = 25.98 ≈ 26.0 m. The key insight: horizontal speed never changes throughout the entire motion!
