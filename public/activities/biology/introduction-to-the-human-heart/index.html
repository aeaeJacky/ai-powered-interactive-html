Introduction to the Human Heart

Meet the powerful muscle that never stops working — from the moment you were born until your very last heartbeat.

1 Overview & Core Functions

Your heart is a muscular pump about the size of your fist.

  • 🫀
    Pumps blood around the bodyThe heart works as a double pump, sending blood on two connected journeys: to the lungs and to the rest of the body.
  • 💨
    Delivers oxygen & nutrientsBlood carries life-giving oxygen and nutrients to every cell, and collects carbon dioxide (CO₂) and waste to be removed.
  • 🛡️
    Protects with one-way valvesValves inside the heart keep blood flowing in a single direction, preventing it from flowing backwards.
  • ⏱️
    Adapts to your body's needsThe heart speeds up during exercise or danger and slows down while you rest, adjusting automatically.

3 The Four Chambers

The heart has four chambers — two atria (top) that receive blood and two ventricles (bottom) that pump blood out.

🔵

Right Atrium

Receives deoxygenated blood

Collects oxygen-poor blood returning from the body via the vena cava, then passes it down to the right ventricle.

🔵

Right Ventricle

Pumps deoxygenated blood

Pumps the deoxygenated blood through the pulmonary arteries to the lungs to pick up fresh oxygen.

🔴

Left Atrium

Receives oxygenated blood

Receives freshly oxygenated blood from the lungs via the pulmonary veins and passes it to the left ventricle.

🔴

Left Ventricle

Pumps oxygenated blood

The strongest chamber — its thick muscular wall pumps oxygen-rich blood out through the aorta to the whole body.

5 Valves & Major Vessels

Valves ensure blood never flows backwards; vessels are the transport network carrying blood in and out.

💖 The Four Heart Valves

  • Tricuspid ValveBetween right atrium & right ventricle.
  • Pulmonary ValveBetween right ventricle & pulmonary artery (to lungs).
  • Mitral ValveBetween left atrium & left ventricle.
  • Aortic ValveBetween left ventricle & aorta (to body).

🩸 Major Blood Vessels

  • AortaLargest artery — carries oxygenated blood from the heart to the body.
  • Vena CavaTwo large veins returning deoxygenated blood from upper & lower body.
  • Pulmonary ArteriesCarry deoxygenated blood from heart to lungs.
  • Pulmonary VeinsReturn oxygenated blood from lungs to heart.
💡 Memory tip: Remember that the pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood — the reverse of the normal pattern! This is unique to the lung circuit.
